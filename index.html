Five useful first steps

You do not need a large IT team to improve basic security. Start with the accounts and information your business relies on most. Follow the linked NCSC guidance for details tailored to your organisation.

1

Secure email and important accounts

Turn on passkeys where available. Otherwise use a strong, unique password and two-step verification. Prioritise email because it can be used to reset other accounts.

NCSC: secure your email
2

Keep devices protected and updated

Use a strong device lock, change default passwords and install operating-system, application and firmware updates on a regular schedule.

NCSC: protect your devices
3

Back up the data you need to operate

Make backups, keep a copy disconnected when practical, protect cloud backups and test that important files can actually be restored.

NCSC: back up your data
4

Help staff spot suspicious messages

Pause before acting on unexpected requests, links or attachments. Verify unusual payment or account-change requests using a trusted contact method.

NCSC: spot cyber attacks
5

Make a simple incident plan

Know who to contact, how to isolate an affected device and where your backups are. Use the free NCSC Cyber Action Toolkit to identify suitable next actions.

Next step

Consider Cyber Essentials

If you need a recognised baseline, learn about Cyber Essentials and its requirements from the official scheme. Certification and assessment may involve fees.

NCSC: Cyber Essentials overview
Start small: choose one high-impact action today — for example, enable two-step verification on the business email account, then check that backups can be restored.
